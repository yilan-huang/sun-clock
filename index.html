Temporal (seasonal) hours — ancient Greek & Roman. Daylight is divided into 12 hours and night into 12, counted from sunrise (0) to sunset (12), so an "hour" stretches in summer and shrinks in winter — about 45 min at midwinter and 75 min at midsummer at this latitude. The sun peaks at the end of the 6th hour: that midday hour, hora sexta, gave us siesta, while the mid-afternoon 9th, nona, later drifted to mean noon. (Jewish sha'ah zmanit still keeps time this way.)

equinoctial clock

Equal (equinoctial) hours are 1⁄24 of the day, every one the same length, zeroed at midnight — the modern clock. Count the same equal hours from a solar event instead and you get the sundial systems the readout above echoes: Babylonian hours (from sunrise) and Italian hours (from sunset).
