Temporal (seasonal) hours — ancient Greek & Roman. Daylight was split into 12 hours and night into 12, so an "hour" stretched in summer and shrank in winter. Here on the Aegean a summer daytime hour runs ~75 min, a winter one ~45. Midday always begins the 7th hour; the 6th — hora sexta — gave us both siesta and noon.
relative to the sun
Babylonian & Italian hours. Babylonian hours counted up from sunrise; Italian (and Bohemian) hours counted up from sunset, rolling over at sundown. Both read the day by its solar edges — the practical ancestor of this view, still echoed in Islamic prayer times and the Jewish sha'ah zmanit.

Equal (equinoctial) hours — the modern fixed hour. Standardised once mechanical clocks arrived, because gears couldn't easily vary an hour's length. Convenient and universal, but detached from the actual sky.
